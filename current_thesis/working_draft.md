DISCUSSION
Generally, between first and second line drugs, a decrease in the number of significant variants associated with resistance can be observed. This can largely be attributed to the drug application timeframe and ubiquity. First- line drugs were first introduced in the mid 20th century, allowing ~70 years of selective pressure. Along side M.tb's clonal and fast evolving nature, resistance mutations accumulated in direct target genes, compensatory loci, and distant epistatic genes. In contrast, second-line drugs follow a more recent trajectory, allowing lesser time for resistance acquisition. This is reflected in plot sparsity. In cases where filtered variants or annotated variants can be observed for second-line drugs, increased attention should be partitioned. As these variants are much more likely to confer resistance, due to the unsaturated resistance landscape.

The annotated candidates tell a compelling narrative: TB-Profiler, a sophisticated tool with an augmented WHO catalogue, lineage tracking mechanisms, and epistatis accounting heuristics, failed to classify these samples correctly. From those missed resistant samples, uncatalogued variants were identified in established drug target genes, passed statistical filtering, lineage exclusion, absent from existing catalogues, and were found to improve sensitivity. The convergence of the aforementioned factors strongly posits a case for further validation.

Delamanid (DLM) produced 320 significant positive associations with 20 variants passing the significance threshold and 18 passing the full filtering protocol. A PPE-54 SNP (Position = 3733795, C>T, FNP = 14%, MRS = 1%) was observed in 14% of all DLM resistant samples. No variants had met the ablation threshold.

DISCUSSION
Given DLM is a newer drug in anti-TB treatment, there were a substantial number of filtered variants observed. The lack of annotations can likely be attributed to the scattered variants over many loci, rather than considerable FN prediction ability by individual variants. This is a similar pattern to LZD and PZA, suggesting more DLM resistant cases will improve numbers for resistance-conferred variants.

Considering DLM metabolism, the DLM prodrug activated via the $F_{420}$ coenzyme pathway. Following activation the drug becomes unstable, releasing Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS), damaging the bacteria. DLM resistance is followed from loss-of-function mutations in the F420 activation pathway which encompasses multiple loci ($ddn, fbiA, fbiB, fbiC, fgd1, and Rv2983$). This multi-gene architecture supports the lack of annotated variants, as prediction accuracy is accumulated over variants in numerous genes. 

Lastly, there is a filtered $PPE-54$ variant (Position = 3733795, C>T) that is distinct from first-line $PPE-54$ variants. However, with the variant being present in only 14$\%$ of all DLM FN samples and out off all DLM-resistant samples only 1$\%$ is missing the variant. This suggests, the variant is overwhelmingly present in TN samples, and its inclusion in a variant catalogue improving predictions would be unlikely.  

Although Pretomanid (PA) was absent from the dataset, DLM-PA cross-resistance knowledge can be exploited to extrapolate PA's resistance profile. As both drugs attack the F420 activation pathway, variants that confer resistance for DLM can be assumed to cause resistance in PA sample, at least in the interim before further data is available. This is approach mimics the WHO expert rules, where cross-resistance between drugs promotes variants that were not associated with resistance (group 3) to associated with resistance in the interim (group 2). Therefore, the 18 filtered variants from DLM FN samples should also be used for PA-resistance investigations. This importance is exacerbated by the inclusion of DLM and PA in BPaLM, and their recent approval for treatment.
